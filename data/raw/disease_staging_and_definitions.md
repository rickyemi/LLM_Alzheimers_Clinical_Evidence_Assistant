# Biological and clinical staging
Alzheimer's disease can be described biologically, by the presence of amyloid and tau pathology, and clinically, by the stage of impairment. The clinical continuum runs from cognitively unimpaired, through mild cognitive impairment (MCI), to mild, moderate and severe dementia.

# Mild cognitive impairment
MCI describes measurable cognitive decline that does not significantly interfere with independence in daily activities. Not every person with MCI has Alzheimer's disease pathology, and biomarkers help identify those who do.

# Amyloid and tau
Amyloid plaques and tau tangles are the hallmark pathologies. Amyloid changes appear early, often years before symptoms. Tau accumulation tracks more closely with symptom onset and progression.

# Core biomarkers
Biomarkers of amyloid (PET, CSF Abeta42/40, plasma p-tau217) and of tau pathology are considered core markers. Non-specific markers such as NfL indicate injury but not its cause.
