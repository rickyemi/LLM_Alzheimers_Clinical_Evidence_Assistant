# Two-cutoff approach
Many assays report results using two cutoffs, which creates a low band, an intermediate band and a high band. Results below the lower cutoff are considered biomarker negative. Results at or above the upper cutoff are considered biomarker positive.

# Intermediate zone
Results between the two cutoffs fall in an intermediate zone where accuracy is lower. Intermediate results should be confirmed with CSF analysis or amyloid PET rather than acted on alone. The width of the intermediate zone is chosen during validation to reach a target accuracy outside the zone.

# Why two cutoffs
A single cutoff forces every result into positive or negative, even when the value sits close to the threshold. Using two cutoffs reduces misclassification at the cost of a share of patients needing further testing.

# Reporting
Reports should state the assay name, the cutoffs, the band, and a reminder that the result requires clinical correlation.
