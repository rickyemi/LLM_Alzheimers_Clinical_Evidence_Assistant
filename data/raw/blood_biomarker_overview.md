# Overview
Blood-based biomarkers (BBMs) measure proteins in plasma that reflect Alzheimer's disease pathology. They are less invasive and less costly than cerebrospinal fluid (CSF) sampling or amyloid PET imaging, which makes broader access possible.

# Key analytes
Plasma phosphorylated tau 217 (p-tau217) is among the most informative markers of amyloid-related pathology. The ratio of amyloid beta 42 to amyloid beta 40 (Abeta42/40) in plasma is another widely studied marker. Neurofilament light chain (NfL) reflects neuro-axonal injury and is not specific to Alzheimer's disease. Glial fibrillary acidic protein (GFAP) reflects astrocyte activation.

# Role in the diagnostic workup
BBMs support a clinical evaluation and do not replace it. A result is interpreted together with symptoms, examination, cognitive testing and imaging. Plasma markers of amyloid pathology can help decide who needs confirmatory CSF or PET testing.

# Limitations
Plasma biomarker levels can be affected by kidney function and other comorbidities. Assays differ in performance, so cutoffs are assay specific and must not be transferred between platforms.
